## 3.6 RF predictive skill and spatial robustness

To attribute the spatially distributed responses of the hydrological states and fluxes to specific hydroclimatic and surface drivers, a Random Forest (RF) regression model was utilized. The predictive skill of the RF model highlights varying degrees of association depending on the target variable. The evaluation was performed using both a temporal split (training on 2016–2019, testing on 2020) and a rigorous spatial block cross-validation (5-Fold GroupKFold) to assess spatial generalization. 

ET and RZSM are the most predictable DA-induced responses and show the strongest spatial generalization (Spatial CV R² > 0.65). This high skill indicates a strong, consistent relationship between ET/RZSM and their corresponding hydroclimatic predictors. Surface increments (SSM) are moderately predictable, maintaining spatial CV R² scores around 0.40. In contrast, the predictability of the CDF/NoCDF contrast is weakly to moderately predictable, suggesting that the non-linear rescaling process obscures some of the direct statistical associations with static and forcing variables. Notably, while baseflow showed temporal predictability, its response is not spatially robust, as evidenced by a failure to generalize in the spatial CV framework. This lack of spatial robustness limits the interpretation of the baseflow response, suggesting that its variations are highly localized and not governed by broadly applicable spatial rules.

## 3.7 RF attribution of assimilation responses

Feature importance analysis was conducted to identify the dominant predictors associated with the assimilation-induced changes. Predictors were assessed individually and grouped into physical families: initial states, hydrological fluxes (OPL), meteorological forcing, static surface properties, and seasonality. 

The grouped importance reveals that the initial soil moisture state and OPL fluxes are critically important for the RZSM and ET responses. This suggests that the magnitude of the assimilation impact on ET and RZSM is strongly associated with the system's prior state. Furthermore, static properties such as land_cover and soil_texture are consistently associated with spatial response structures. While these variables are important predictors in the RF model, it must be emphasized that the RF identifies statistical associations and does not prove causal mechanisms. For instance, land_cover and soil_texture are associated with the spatial structure of DA responses, suggesting that unresolved or imperfectly represented land-surface heterogeneity may modulate assimilation impacts. Ultimately, the RF attribution is consistent with the interpretation that the DA-induced changes are physically consistent redistributions driven by forcing and initial states, providing a diagnostic lens rather than replacing the physical simulations. The RF/XAI analysis is an attribution layer that identifies statistical associations between predictors and DA-induced responses. It does not replace Noah-MP physics, does not prove causality, and does not provide water-balance closure. The flux-only water-balance diagnostic remains a separate first-order hydrological check.
